# Dual-gate FLIM cardiac electrophysiology

Work in `/app` with the supplied `data/` and write your submission to `output/`.

Analyze the supplied simulated dual-gate SPAD photon counts for hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. The source package describes a 500 × 500 acquisition at 192 frames/s, but the supplied text contains sparse sampled coordinates and frames. Measure its actual coverage. Report what those observations support and mark unestimable quantities explicitly. Do not synthesize missing observations or infer significance from unavailable data.

Use the raw count pairs in `data/raw_flim_stack.txt`, calibration points in `calibration_kv.txt`, static intensity guidance in `mask_intensity.txt`, nominal stimulation events in `stim_info.csv`, and instrument constants in `params_config.txt`. FOVs 1–4 correspond to quadrants split at x=250 and y=250. Identify dark samples by signal and quality, adapt denoising to local signal-to-noise, derive lifetime from the exponential decay and dual gates, and fit a justified linear or spline lifetime-to-voltage calibration. Flag saturated, low-confidence and physiologically implausible samples. Attempt beat alignment from observed signals, quantify APD30/APD90 and late repolarization where possible, and separate field-of-view comparisons and statistical uncertainty. Use open scientific Python tools and the supplied inputs.

Write `preprocessed_flim_stack.txt`, `tau_timeseries.txt`, `Vm_mapping.txt`, `ap_waveform.csv`, `stat_ap_ratio.csv`, `fig1_rmp_data.txt`, `fig2_ap_trace_data.txt`, `fig3_apd_boxplot_data.txt`, `fig4_vm_hist_data.txt` and `analysis_conclusion.md` to `output/`. Use comma-delimited values for coordinate tables: frame, x, y, corrected gate counts and confidence; frame, x, y, lifetime in ns and error; or frame, x, y, voltage in mV, confidence and saturation flag, respectively. For beat and FOV tables include identifiers, APD30/90, ratio, slow window and confidence as appropriate. Use explicit missing values for unsupported measurements. Discuss interpolation, adaptive filtering, motion, the possible role of NCX and error limits. The original reference files contain illustrative values and must be interpreted in light of input coverage.
