# Raman spectroscopy of Fe-doped monolayer MoS₂

English translation of the original grading rubric. Keep this document out of the blind solver environment.

The rubric assigns 100 points based on the submitted figure, CSV and report. The grading does not depend on software choice or implementation. There is no reference figure. Assess scientific content and verifiability without scoring visual style. Reasonable peak shapes, baselines, fit windows and statistical metrics can yield slightly different values.

## 1. Sample comparison and key spectral features (20)

- Identify the main doping-induced changes at intermediate and low frequencies, and establish that Fe:MoS₂ requires more components than undoped MoS₂ (7).
- Locate the existing hybridized mode near 265 cm⁻¹. Reference 264.92 ± 0.25 cm⁻¹ (4).
- Locate the additional doping-induced component near 283 cm⁻¹. Reference 282.86 ± 0.19 cm⁻¹ (5).
- Support the extra component with residuals, model comparison, parameter stability or equivalent evidence beyond a local maximum (4).

Cap this section at 6 if the submission only analyzes the conventional 380–500 cm⁻¹ modes and misses the central changes near 265 and 283 cm⁻¹.

## 2. Field and circular polarization response (25)

- Physically track both sets of components continuously from −7 to +7 T (8).
- Identify the relation between RL and LR near zero field and how their polarization-dependent shifts exchange on reversing the field (6).
- Support the approximately linear field shifts for both sets with slope significance, residuals or uncertainty (5).
- Distinguish polarization-dependent frequency shifts from common drift, intensity-only changes, fit failure or swapped branch identities (4).
- Handle outliers, fits at parameter bounds or unstable branch tracking, or clearly state the corresponding limitations (2).

Tracking the high-frequency mode near 450 cm⁻¹ alone is insufficient to conclude there is no field response in the relevant branches.

## 3. Effective phonon magnetic moment (15)

- For the existing component near 265 cm⁻¹, recover about 2.0–2.5 μB. Reference estimates are 2.14 ± 0.07 and 2.29 ± 0.07 μB (5).
- For the additional component near 283 cm⁻¹, recover about 2.5–3.1 μB. Reference estimates are 2.82 ± 0.09 and 2.76 ± 0.06 μB (5).
- Use consistent definitions, unit conversion and the factor of two relating a single branch shift to the splitting between two branches (3).
- Report uncertainty and use it to assess agreement between polarization channels (2).

Near-zero estimates for all branches due to tracking the wrong modes do not satisfy this section.

## 4. Low-frequency continuum and temperature response (15)

- Construct a measure related to static susceptibility using the thermal population correction and the Kramers–Kronig 1/ω weight. Integrating raw intensity or unweighted χ″ does not satisfy this criterion (6).
- Identify and quantify the overall growth of low-frequency spectral weight on cooling (4).
- Use a physically motivated temperature model and compare it with simpler alternatives using residuals, information criteria, parameter stability or equivalent evidence (3).
- Check sensitivity to at least one of the low-frequency cutoff, integration window or background. Explain how the finite frequency window limits model parameters (2).

No single Curie–Weiss temperature Θ is required. The finite-window reference analysis obtained a negative Θ of order −20 K, with dependence on the window and background.

## 5. Physical interpretation and evidential limits (20)

- Integrate the additional doping-induced mode, polarization-dependent shifts of both branches, effective phonon magnetic moments and low-temperature continuum into a consistent interpretation (8).
- Interpret the effective moment as the field-coupling scale of the Raman or phonon branch. It is not a direct measurement of static bulk magnetization (3).
- Relate the continuum to a compatible local magnetic response or spin-fluctuation interpretation consistent with the field dependence (3).
- Distinguish evidence of magneto-Raman and low-frequency magnetic response from proof of a particular zero-field long-range magnetic order (4).
- State which magnetic-ground-state questions require independent magnetic characterization (2).

Deduct substantially if Zeeman-like splitting, a finite effective moment or the continuum is directly equated with long-range ferromagnetic or antiferromagnetic order.

## 6. Completeness and consistency (5)

- A clear 300 dpi figure supports the sample comparison and the field, polarization and temperature claims (2).
- The CSV provides main quantitative results, experimental conditions, uncertainties and units (2).
- The report agrees with the quantitative results and conclusions in the figure and CSV (1).
