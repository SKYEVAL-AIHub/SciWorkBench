## 2. Quantum rate theory

The electronic and nuclear Hamiltonian is

$$
\hat H(r,R)=\hat T(R)+\hat H_{\mathrm{el}}(r;R).
$$

The surface $s(R)=0$ separates reactants ($s(R)<0$) and products ($s(R)>0$). The quantum rate is the long-time Kubo-transformed flux-side correlation function divided by the reactant partition function.

$$
k_{\mathrm{QM}}=
\frac{1}{Q_A}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}C_{fs}(T,t),
$$

$$
C_{fs}(T,t)=
\int_0^\beta\frac{d\lambda}{\beta}
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
e^{-(\beta-\lambda)\hat H}
\hat F
e^{-\lambda\hat H}
e^{i\hat Ht}
\hat h
e^{-i\hat Ht}
\right].
$$

$\hat h=h[s(R)]$ is the product projection and $\hat F=i[\hat H,\hat h]$ is the flux operator.

Adiabatic electronic states satisfy

$$
\hat H_{\mathrm{el}}(r;R)\phi_i(r;R)
=V_i(R)\phi_i(r;R).
$$

The adiabatic approximation retains only the ground PES. The nuclear Hamiltonian is

$$
\hat H_{\mathrm{ad}}=\hat T(R)+\hat V_0(R).
$$

For a one-dimensional free gas-phase reactant, the translational partition function per unit length is

$$
Q_A=\left(\frac{M}{2\pi\beta}\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Use exact quantum scattering on the ground PES for `exact_adiabatic` and on the full two-state Hamiltonian for `exact_nonadiabatic`.

## 3. Classical FSSH

Expand the electronic wavefunction in the instantaneous adiabatic basis:

$$
\psi(r,R,t)=\sum_i c_i(t)\phi_i(r;R).
$$

Electronic amplitudes obey Eq. 17:

$$
i\dot c_i=V_i(R)c_i
-i\sum_j\dot R\,d_{ij}(R)c_j,
$$

The derivative coupling is Eq. 18:

$$
d_{ij}(R)=
\langle\phi_i(r;R)|\nabla_R|\phi_j(r;R)\rangle.
$$

Define

$$
a_{ij}=c_ic_j^*,
$$

$$
b_{ji}=-2\operatorname{Re}
\left(a_{ji}^*\dot R\,d_{ji}\right).
$$

The FSSH probability for a hop from active state i to j is Eq. 19:

$$
g_{ij}=\frac{\Delta t\,b_{ji}}{a_{ii}},
$$

Clamp negative probabilities to zero. On a hop, switch the active surface and adjust nuclear momentum along the nonadiabatic coupling direction to conserve energy. Reject energetically inaccessible hops.

The classical nonadiabatic rate combines multiple-PES transition-state flux with state-selected transmission coefficients. The indices in, i and fi denote the asymptotic reactant state, dividing-surface state and asymptotic product state.

## 4. Adiabatic RPMD

The P-bead Hamiltonian in Eq. 29 is

$$
H_P^{\mathrm{ad}}=
\sum_{k=1}^{P}\frac{p_k^2}{2M}
+\sum_{k=1}^{P}\frac{M\omega_P^2}{2}(R_{k+1}-R_k)^2
+\sum_{k=1}^{P}V(R_k),
$$

where

$$
R_{P+1}=R_1,
\qquad
\beta_P=\frac{\beta}{P},
\qquad
\omega_P=\frac{1}{\beta_P}=\frac{P}{\beta}.
$$

The centroid position and momentum are

$$
\bar R=\frac1P\sum_{k=1}^{P}R_k,
\qquad
\bar p=\frac1P\sum_{k=1}^{P}p_k.
$$

Sample the extended ring-polymer phase space with $e^{-\beta_PH_P}$. The dividing surface depends on $\bar R$. The short-time limit gives centroid QTST:

$$
k_{\mathrm{QTST}}=
\frac{1}{Q_A^P}
\int\frac{d\mathbf R\,d\mathbf p}{(2\pi)^P}
e^{-\beta_PH_P}
\delta(\bar R-R^\ddagger)
\frac{\bar p}{M}h(\bar p).
$$

## 5. Ring-polymer surface hopping

### 5.1 Centroid approximation

The CA electronic equation is Eq. 35:

$$
i\dot c_i=V_i(\bar R)c_i
-i\sum_j\dot{\bar R}\,d_{ij}(\bar R)c_j.
$$

CA illustrates the origin of BA and the P=1 limit. The requested rates use BA.

### 5.2 Bead approximation

All beads share one active adiabatic PES and switch surfaces together at a hop.

The BA electronic equation is Eq. 36:

$$
i\dot c_i=
\left[\frac1P\sum_{k=1}^{P}V_i(R_k)\right]c_i
-i\sum_j
\left[\frac1P\sum_{k=1}^{P}
\dot R_kd_{ij}(R_k)\right]c_j.
$$

Harmonic springs between neighboring beads do not appear in the electronic equation because they do not affect relative electronic phases. BA approaches CA as the polymer contracts at high temperature.

Nuclear dynamics are generated by the ring-polymer Hamiltonian on active surface i:

$$
H_P(\mathbf p,\mathbf R;i)=
\sum_{k=1}^{P}\frac{p_k^2}{2M}
+\sum_{k=1}^{P}\frac{M\omega_P^2}{2}(R_{k+1}-R_k)^2
+\sum_{k=1}^{P}V_i(R_k).
$$

At a BA hop, adjust bead momenta along the multidimensional vector of bead derivative couplings to conserve the entire ring-polymer Hamiltonian. Reject hops when kinetic energy is insufficient.

The RPSH flux-side correlation function is

$$
C_{fs}^{\mathrm{RPSH}}(T,t)=
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^P}
\sum_i\int d\mathbf R_0d\mathbf p_0
e^{-\beta_PH_P(\mathbf R_0,\mathbf p_0;i)}
\delta(\bar R_0-R^\ddagger)
\frac{\bar p_0}{M}
h(\bar R_t-R^\ddagger).
$$

The rate is

$$
k_{\mathrm{RPSH}}=
\frac1{Q_A^P}
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}
C_{fs}^{\mathrm{RPSH}}(T,t).
$$

The state-selected form is Eq. 40:

$$
k_{\mathrm{RPSH}}=
\frac1{Q_A^P}
\sum_{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{fi},i}
C_{fs,i}^{P}(T,0^+)
\kappa_{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{fi}}^i.
$$

## 6. MPES-QTST

Eq. 41 defines

$$
k_{\mathrm{MPES-QTST}}=
\frac1{Q_A^P}\sum_i C_{fs,i}^{P}(T,0^+).
$$

For rare-event sampling, choose a centroid surface $\bar R_0$ in the reactant asymptote and a variational surface $\bar R_1$ in the interaction region. Eq. 42 gives

$$
k_{\mathrm{MPES-QTST}}=
\sum_i k_i(\bar R_0)
p_i(\bar R_1,\bar R_0).
$$

The one-dimensional model has two degenerate electronic states in the reactant asymptote, so Eq. 45 gives

$$
k_i(\bar R_0)=
\frac12(2\pi M\beta)^{-1/2}.
$$

The flux ratio equals the ratio of centroid probabilities at the two surfaces:

$$
p_i(\bar R_1,\bar R_0)=\frac{P_i(1)}{P_i(0)},
$$

$$
P_i(\xi)=
\frac{
\int d\mathbf R\,d\mathbf p\,e^{-\beta_PH_P(\mathbf R,\mathbf p;i)}
\delta(\bar R-\bar R_\xi)
}{
\int d\mathbf R\,d\mathbf p\,e^{-\beta_PH_P(\mathbf R,\mathbf p;i)}
}.
$$

Define the potential of mean force from Eq. 48:

$$
A_i(\xi)=-\frac1{\beta_P}\ln P_i(\xi),
$$

Then Eq. 49 gives

$$
p_i(\bar R_1,\bar R_0)=
e^{-\beta_P[A_i(1)-A_i(0)]}.
$$

Use umbrella integration with overlapping harmonic-restraint windows between $\bar R_0$ and $\bar R_1$. Run constant-temperature ring-polymer sampling on each surface and at each temperature. Recover the mean force from the first two moments of the centroid distribution in each window.

## 7. State-selected transmission coefficient and backward rare-event algorithm

Eq. 50 defines

$$
\kappa_{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{fi}}^i=
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}
\frac{
\int d\mathbf R_0d\mathbf p_0\,e^{-\beta_PH_P(\mathbf R_0,\mathbf p_0;i)}
\delta(\bar R_0-R^\ddagger)
\delta_{\mathrm{in},n(-t)}
\frac{\bar p_0}{M}
h(\bar R_t-R^\ddagger)
\delta_{\mathrm{fi},n(t)}
}{
C_{fs,i}^{P}(T,0^+;R^\ddagger)
}.
$$

The procedure follows Sec. VI B:

1. Fix the centroid to the dividing surface in the interaction region and perform constant-temperature ring-polymer sampling on surface i with centroid motion removed.
2. Draw new Maxwell momenta for independent bead configurations.
3. Propagate constant-energy RPSH backward and forward from each phase-space point.
4. Use physical bead masses. The multiple-time-step integrator alternates free-ring evolution and PES force updates.
5. Propagate electronic amplitudes with RK4, updating the electronic Hamiltonian at the beginning, midpoint and end of the long nuclear step.

For stability at large energy gaps, factor out the adiabatic phase. The CA interaction-picture amplitude is Eq. 51:

$$
\tilde c_i(t)=c_i(t)
\exp\left[i\int_{-\infty}^{t}V_i(t')dt'\right],
$$

It obeys Eq. 52:

$$
\dot{\tilde c}_i=
-\sum_j\dot{\bar R}d_{ij}
\exp\left[-i\int_{-\infty}^{t}(V_j-V_i)dt'\right]
\tilde c_j.
$$

BA uses the bead-averaged $V_i$ and $\dot R_kd_{ij}$ terms from Eq. 36.

The dividing surface lies in the coupling region, where the electronic amplitudes are initially unknown. Use the trial backward surface-hopping probability of Eq. 53:

$$
f_{ij}=\frac12
\left[1-exp\left(-
\left|\frac1P\sum_k\dot R_kd_{ij}(R_k)\right|
\Delta t\right)
\right].
$$

Propagate two independent electronic solutions on the same backward nuclear path and retain the electronic Hamiltonian. In the reactant asymptote, where coupling vanishes, apply Eq. 54 for the specified incoming state:

$$
c_{\mathrm{in}}(-\infty)=1,
\qquad
c_{j\ne\mathrm{in}}(-\infty)=0.
$$

The asymptotic condition uniquely determines a linear combination of the backward solutions. Use it to obtain dividing-surface amplitudes, reconstruct amplitudes along the backward path and evaluate the FSSH target probabilities.

The backward-path reweighting factor is Eq. 55:

$$
W=\prod_\mu w_\mu.
$$

If step $\mu$ attempts an $i\to j$ switch, use

$$
w_\mu=\frac{g_{ij}}{f_{ij}}.
$$

If no switch is attempted, use Eq. 56:

$$
w_\mu=
\frac{1-\sum_{j\ne i}g_{ij}}
{1-\sum_{j\ne i}f_{ij}}.
$$

Propagate forward from the inferred dividing-surface electronic state using normal FSSH probabilities. With both backward and forward propagation, use positive outward initial centroid momenta. Divide each reactive trajectory's contribution by its number of forward crossings. The state-selected transmission coefficient is the mean reactive flux divided by the total thermal flux at the dividing surface.

## 8. One-dimensional two-state model

The diabatic potential elements are Eq. 57:

$$
V_{11}=A e^{-BR^2},
$$

$$
V_{22}=C e^{2E(R-D)}+2C e^{E(R-D)},
$$

$$
V_{12}=F e^{-GR^2}.
$$

All parameters below are in atomic units:

$$
M=2000,
\quad A=5\times10^{-3},
\quad B=2,
\quad C=10^{-5},
$$

$$
D=-2.8,
\quad E=1.2,
\quad F=10^{-3},
\quad G=2.
$$

Diagonalize the diabatic matrix at each coordinate to obtain the two adiabatic PESs. The ground surface has a barrier and the excited surface is repulsive. The two states are degenerate in the negative-R asymptote and the coupling vanishes at large absolute R. Put the dividing surface at the lower-surface barrier top with reactants to the left.

## 9. Computational details from the Appendix

### 9.1 Exact quantum rates

The paper obtains exact rates by integrating squared constant-energy scattering amplitudes. It uses Feit–Fleck split-operator Fourier wave-packet propagation extended to multiple PESs:

- Initialize the wave packet in the distant reactant region.
- Propagate through the scattering event.
- Absorb reflected components in the reactant asymptote.
- Project the transmitted component in the product asymptote.
- Fourier transform the transmitted fraction and normalize by flux.

The paper covers nine temperatures from 50 to 5000 K. Reproduce 143 K and 250 K.

### 9.2 Beads, QTST sampling and time step

The paper uses 32 beads from 143 K upward and 128 beads at 100 K and below. Use 32 for both target temperatures.

MPES-QTST uses 100 umbrella windows, from $-3a_0$ to $3a_0$ for the ground PES and from $-3a_0$ to $0.5a_0$ for the excited PES. The harmonic force constant is

$$
1.22\ \mathrm{eV}/a_0^2.
$$

Equilibrate each window for $2.5\times10^5$ steps and sample for $10^7$ steps in the published calculation. Centroid-mode friction is twice the umbrella frequency. The published long step at 143 K and 250 K is

$$
2.4\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{fs}.
$$

### 9.3 Transmission sampling

The paper equilibrates for $10^6$ steps, then takes a constrained ring-polymer configuration every $2.5\times10^3$ steps with fresh Maxwell momenta. It runs $10^6$ forward/backward trajectories per PES and temperature.

At 250 K and below, the free-ring integration step is one tenth of the long step. Classical surface hopping uses P=1. Adiabatic RPMD propagates on the ground PES in both directions.
